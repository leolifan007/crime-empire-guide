Three game modes in Crime Simulator, and I tried all of them before settling on my preferred way to play. Here is how they actually differ.

**Standard** -- Normal difficulty, no permadeath, quotas enabled. This is where I learned the game. You can die and restart without losing everything. The game saves progress at the hideout.

**Hardcore** -- Permadeath. If you get arrested, the save is gone. Quotas are higher (roughly 1.5x based on community reports), police are more aggressive, and fence prices are lower by about 20%. I attempted it twice and died twice before I was ready. The Steam discussions confirm this is the consensus -- Hardcore demands full map knowledge first.

**Completionist** -- No quota pressure, variable difficulty, no permadeath. This is the achievement hunting mode. I use it when going for specific unlocks without worrying about the daily timer. The freedom to take all the time you need makes a big difference for hard achievements.

**Quota system:**

The quota is a timer-driven progression system. You get a minimum dollar target for a set number of in-game days. Meet it, you rank up. Miss it, you get penalties -- higher police attention, worse fence prices, eventually a game over condition.

If you keep failing quotas, stop chasing volume. I learned this the hard way. Focus on high-value items (electronics, jewelry, gold bars) instead of grabbing everything. One laptop is worth five kitchen appliances and takes the same inventory slot.

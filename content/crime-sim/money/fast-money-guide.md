The fastest first $1,000 I found: complete the tutorial, grab the crowbar, rob the nearest low-security house, sell electronics at the fence, buy a lockpick set. Repeat with better entry on house number two. Took me about 20 minutes of real time.

The early game is about capital accumulation. Every dollar goes back into tools that enable better heists. I wasted my first payout on hideout decorations and regretted it for the next hour.

**Early game money loop (first hour):**

**Step 1:** Do the tutorial. It gives you starter cash and the crowbar. I tried skipping it once and ended up confused about lockpicking mechanics.

**Step 2:** Target the three cheapest houses from the job board. These have no electronic locks, fewer NPC patrols, and lower police response time. I hit all three in one loop.

**Step 3:** Focus on electronics and small valuables. A laptop is worth roughly 5x what a kitchen appliance sells for and takes the same inventory slot. I leave blenders and toasters behind now.

**Step 4:** Sell at the fence, buy a lockpick set and flashlight. The lockpick set pays for itself within two heists. The flashlight prevents you from missing high-value items in dark corners.

**What I do differently now:**

After the first few runs, I stopped selling everything immediately. I hold onto items that match active requests from the request board. A single requested item can pay $350-$400 instead of $200 at the fence. Multiple requests on the same item type stack.

Steam community guides confirm the same pattern: complete jobs first for guaranteed cash, sell duplicate skill leaflets for premium, then fence the rest. Gold bars from Update 1.3 should never be sold early -- they fund basement upgrades that unlock better contracts.

Your hideout is more than just a place to store loot. It is your operations center, your safe zone, and the key to long-term progression. Choosing the right upgrades and managing your storage effectively can double your earning potential. The [loot locations guide](/crime-sim/loot/loot-locations-and-values/) helps you stock what actually sells.

## Hideout Basics

Your hideout serves several critical functions:
- **Storage:** Keep loot between runs for item requests or bulk selling
- **Safe zone:** Police cannot enter your hideout -- get here and you are safe
- **Save point:** The game autosaves when you visit your hideout
- **Job board:** Accept heist contracts and item requests
- **Upgrades:** Basement expansion (Update 1.3) unlocks powerful bonuses

## Storage Management

Your hideout has limited storage space. Managing it well is essential.

| Storage Upgrade | Base Capacity | After Storage Rack | After Both |
|:--------------:|:-------------:|:------------------:|:----------:|
| Standard | 20 slots | 30 slots | 40 slots |

Basement upgrades require gold bars -- the [gold bars guide](/crime-sim/money/gold-bars-and-jewelry-guide/) explains the full economy.

{{< callout "tip" >}}
Storage management rule: keep high-value items for requests, sell everything else immediately. Your hideout is not a museum -- it is a staging area. I keep 2-3 laptops, 2-3 jewelry pieces, and 1-2 gold bars. Everything else gets fenced immediately.
{{< /callout >}}

### What to Store vs What to Sell

| Item Type | Store? | Reason |
|-----------|:------:|--------|
| Gold bars | Always | Upgrade currency for basement |
| Authentic jewelry | Yes | High-value request items |
| Laptops/tablets | 2-3 | Common request items |
| Smartphones | 2-3 | Common request items |
| Rare skill leaflets | Until learned | Permanent progression |
| Cash | Always (auto-stored) | Spent at shops |
| Fake jewelry | Never | Low value, fence immediately |
| Kitchen appliances | Never | Too low value to store |
| Clothing | Never | Bulky, low value |
| Tools (backup) | 1 each | Emergency replacements |

## Basement Upgrades (Update 1.3)

Update 1.3 added a hideout basement expansion system. Gold bars (a new resource) are used to purchase upgrades that provide permanent bonuses.

### Upgrade Order

| Priority | Upgrade | Gold Bar Cost | Effect | Why This Order |
|:--------:|---------|:------------:|--------|----------------|
| 1st | Storage Rack | 2 bars | +50% storage (20 -> 30 slots) | More storage = more high-value items held for requests |
| 2nd | Tool Wall | 3 bars | Tool break rate reduced | Saves repair costs, reduces mid-heist failures |
| 3rd | Security Door | 5 bars | Loot safety improved | Protects stash on arrest |
| 4th | Workshop Bench | 8 bars | Craft better tools | Endgame feature, needs 8 bars saved |

{{< insight >}}
The Storage Rack is the highest-impact upgrade per gold bar spent. Two gold bars for +10 storage slots means you can hold 10 more high-value items for request delivery. At an average premium of $100-$200 per request item, that is $1,000-$2,000 extra per game session.
{{< /insight >}}

### How the Security Door Works

The Security Door upgrade protects your stash when you are arrested. Without it, you lose everything in the hideout on arrest (Hardcore) or lose a portion (Standard). With the Security Door, your stored loot is protected.

This is a critical upgrade for Hardcore mode players. In Standard mode it is less urgent but still valuable for long-term progression.

## Tool Stash Management

Keeping backup tools is cheaper than buying replacements after a failed heist.

**Recommended backup stash:**
- 1 backup lockpick set (get caught without one and you cannot enter houses)
- 1 backup flashlight (dead flashlight = missed loot)
- 1 sleeping gas (emergency NPC removal)
- 2-3 cable ties (NPC restraint)

**Storage strategy for tools:**
- Dedicate 4-5 storage slots to backup tools
- Rotate tools: when your primary breaks, grab the backup and repair the primary
- Never dip below one backup of each essential tool

## Hideout Layout Tips

While you cannot redesign your hideout, you can organize your storage efficiently:

- **Left side of stash:** High-value items for requests (laptops, jewelry, gold bars)
- **Right side of stash:** Backup tools and equipment
- **Bottom rows:** Bulk items you plan to fence soon
- **Tool wall (if upgraded):** Display your best tools for quick access

## Practical Example: Storage Optimization

Here is my actual stash layout after 10 hours of gameplay:

**Permanent storage (always kept):**
- 2 gold bars (saving for basement upgrades)
- 2 laptops (request items)
- 3 jewelry pieces (request items, authentic)
- 1 set of backup tools (lockpick, flashlight, gas, cables)

**Temporary storage (sold within 2 runs):**
- Any duplicates of requested items
- Extra skill leaflet duplicates
- Mid-value electronics if storage is full

This setup lets me accept any item request that appears, while keeping my stash lean enough to add new loot without needing to sell mid-run.

## Co-op Hideout Sharing

In co-op mode, all players share the same hideout. Storage is shared. This requires coordination:

- Designate one player as the "stash manager" who organizes items
- Pool high-value items for requests before individual selling
- Communicate what you are storing so duplicates are minimized
- Avoid storing items that another player plans to sell immediately

{{< callout "info" >}}
Co-op hideout conflicts are common. I recommend one simple rule: each player gets 5 dedicated storage slots. The rest is shared pool. This prevents one player from filling the stash with low-value items while others have nowhere to store gold bars.
{{< /callout >}}

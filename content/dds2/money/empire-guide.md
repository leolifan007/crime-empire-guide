Scaling past $50,000 in DDS2 requires employees, multiple specialized hideouts, and territory control. The Reddit community organizes by product type -- one hideout for cannabis, one for amphetamine, one for meth. I use this approach and it scales reliably. This guide covers how to transition from a solo operator to a full cartel with automated income streams.

{{< diagram "diagrams/dds2-money-flow.svg" "DDS2 money flow diagram showing production, distribution and sales loop" "720" >}}

**Employee system:**

Employees are unlocked through notice boards (random spawn at bulletin board locations across islands), influencer missions (limited uses per influencer -- typically 2 hires each), and story progression. Once hired, employees can be assigned to different roles.

| Role | Function | Unlock Method | Cost |
|------|----------|--------------|------|
| Dealer | Handle customer orders in assigned territory | Build distributor desk at hideout | ~$2,000 in materials |
| Cook | Auto-craft batches without manual mixing | Upgrade lab station | ~$3,000 in equipment |
| Distributor | Move product between hideouts | Own 2+ hideouts | Cost of second hideout |
| Guard | Reduce raid probability | Build guard station | ~$5,000 |

The distributor desk is the key early milestone. It unlocks the ability to assign dealers, creating a passive income stream that generates money while you focus on higher-value activities. Build this before investing heavily in other upgrades.

**Multi-hideout specialization:**

The optimal mid-game layout based on community strategies and my testing:

| Hideout Location | Product | Purpose | Police Risk |
|-----------------|---------|---------|-------------|
| Jungle | Cannabis | Grow and process, low heat | Low |
| Callejon | Amphetamine | Processing and mixing | Medium |
| Slavs Bay | Meth | High-end cooking | High |
| Coast / Archipelago | Opium | Endgame scaling | Medium |

{{< callout "tip" >}}
Keep high-value production (meth, cocaine) in less conspicuous hideouts. A large lab in a visible downtown location increases raid probability significantly. The Fandom wiki confirms hideout raid risk is affected by both wanted level AND hideout visibility rating.
{{< /callout >}}

**Practical example: Scaling from $5,000 to $50,000 per day**

Here is the step-by-step progression that worked for me:

1. **$5k net worth** -- Buy your second hideout. A rural location at approximately $1,500-4,500 is ideal. Set this up as your raw materials storage with basic processing equipment.
2. **$10k net worth** -- Build a distributor desk at your primary hideout. Hire your first dealer and assign them to a territory you have already built up with client contacts.
3. **$25k net worth** -- Purchase your third hideout, ideally in a different zone than the first two. Install a lab upgrade and hire a cook to automate production. Your dealer should now be generating $2-5k per day passively.
4. **$50k net worth** -- Hire a second dealer for a different territory. Install a guard station at your highest-value hideout to reduce raid risk. Your passive income should reach $10-20k per day.
5. **$100k+** -- Full staff across all roles. Expand to all available territories. Consider co-op play to manage multiple production lines simultaneously.

**Territory progression by net worth:**

| Net Worth | Recommended Action | Passive Income |
|-----------|-------------------|---------------|
| $5k-15k | Buy second hideout, hire first employee | $500-1k/day |
| $15k-50k | Third hideout, hire cook + dealer | $2-5k/day |
| $50k-200k | Full staff, all territories unlocked | $10-20k/day |
| $200k+ | Co-op expansion, Casino DLC content | $20k+/day |

**Income optimization checklist:**

- [ ] Build distributor desk before upgrading lab equipment
- [ ] Assign dealers to territories with established client base
- [ ] Use low-TOX cutting agents (flour, sugar, salt) to maximize volume
- [ ] Keep cash split across multiple hideouts to reduce raid impact
- [ ] Upgrade equipment when production becomes the bottleneck, not before

{{< callout "info" >}}
The community consensus is that organizing hideouts by function rather than by zone is more efficient. Each hideout should specialize in one product type. This reduces setup time because you do not need to move equipment between locations, and it contains losses if a single hideout gets raided.
{{< /callout >}}

Every DDS2 player makes mistakes in their first playthrough. I made most of these myself -- some cost me hours of progress. This guide covers the 10 most common beginner mistakes and how to avoid or recover from each one. Based on my own experience and community discussions from Reddit and Steam.

**1. Keeping everything in one hideout.**

The most common and most costly mistake. A single raid can wipe out your entire operation if everything is in one place. I lost my first major stockpile this way.

| Hideout | Store | Risk Level |
|---------|-------|------------|
| Hideout A | Raw materials, basic equipment | Low value, safe |
| Hideout B | Processing equipment, work-in-progress | Medium value |
| Hideout C | Finished product, cash | High value, visit least |

**Fix:** Own at least 2 hideouts before your net worth exceeds $5,000. Split raw materials, processing, and finished product across different locations. The cost of a second hideout is tiny compared to the loss from a single raid.

**2. Trying to cook before you have clients.**

The tutorial makes cooking seem like the priority, but it is not. In the first 10 hours, deliveries outperform cooking by roughly 3:1. I wasted my first few hours collecting ingredients while having no reliable way to sell finished product.

**Fix:** Focus on ShadyComm deliveries until you have a steady client pipeline in at least one territory. Build relationships with at least 5-10 regular customers before investing in a lab setup. Cooking scales after distribution is established, not before.

**3. Fighting police during raids.**

When a raid triggers, many players instinctively fight back. This is the worst possible response. Police arrive in waves with increasing numbers. Each wave increases your wanted level, making future raids more likely and more intense.

**Fix:** Escape using parkour. Exit through a rear door or window, vault fences, wall-run onto roofs, and hide until the search subsides. Fighting should be your last resort, not your first response. The product you lose is replaceable; a higher wanted level affects everything.

**4. Ignoring the distributor desk.**

The distributor desk unlocks the employee system, which creates passive income. Many new players skip this upgrade in favor of lab equipment or cosmetic items. This slows down their progression significantly.

**Fix:** Build the distributor desk as your first major purchase after the basic lab kit. Cost is approximately $2,000 in materials. It enables dealer assignments, which generate income while you are doing other activities. A single dealer in a developed territory can generate $2-4k per day passively.

**5. Using toxic cutting agents.**

The Datamine catalog shows that some agents have very high toxicity (TOX 0.7-0.9). Using gasoline, methanol, or washing powder as cutting agents may increase volume, but it kills your customers rapidly.

**Fix:** Use only low-TOX cutting agents: flour (TOX 0.0), sugar (TOX 0.0), or salt (TOX 0.0). These provide safe volume increases without harming customer retention. A dead customer is a permanent loss of revenue.

**6. Not using parkour for escape.**

The parkour system is one of DDS2's core mechanics, but many beginners ignore it and try to outrun police on foot or fight them directly. Parkour breaks line of sight instantly and police cannot follow onto most rooftop areas.

**Fix:** Practice the three basic parkour moves: vault (Space at low obstacles), wall-run (Space at walls at an angle), and jump climb (Space at ledges). These three moves cover 90% of escape situations. Keep energy drinks in inventory for extended parkour chains.

**7. Neglecting Street Cred for pure cash grinding.**

Money is important, but Street Cred controls what content you can access. Higher Street Cred unlocks better recipes, more staff slots, larger hideouts, and higher-value customers. Players who focus only on money hit a progression wall.

**Fix:** Spend 20-30 minutes per gaming session actively grinding Street Cred by beating gang members in different territories. This investment pays off by unlocking more profitable content. The balance should be roughly 70% money activities and 30% Street Cred activities in the early game.

**8. Buying the wrong hideout first.**

The first hideout purchase matters. A downtown location with high visibility will attract police attention quickly. A villa might look appealing, but it is a raid magnet.

**Fix:** Buy a rural or jungle hideout as your second location. The cottage near the Archipelago-Callejon border is the community-recommended choice. It has moderate visibility, good access to both zones, and a dock for boat parking. Save the flashy locations for late-game when you have guards and security upgrades.

**9. Ignoring notice boards.**

Notice boards (19 total across all islands) offer employee hiring, side tasks, and bonus rewards. Many beginners never interact with them because they are easy to miss.

**Fix:** Check notice boards whenever you visit a new island. They are usually near market areas or main roads. The bulletin board locations guide on Steam documents all 19 positions. Even if you do not need an employee immediately, checking the board tells you what is available.

**10. Playing solo when co-op is available.**

DDS2 supports 3-person co-op, which dramatically accelerates progression. Solo play is viable but much slower for tasks that benefit from parallel work.

**Fix:** If you have friends playing, coordinate roles rather than duplicating efforts. One player handles cooking and production, one manages deliveries, one manages staff. This triples effective throughput. Resources can be shared between co-op players.

{{< callout "info" >}}
If you have already made some of these mistakes, do not restart your playthrough. All of these issues have recoverable fixes. Split your assets now, build a distributor desk next, and adjust your priorities. The game is forgiving enough that even a major setback can be recovered from in a few hours of focused play.
{{< /callout >}}

**Quick reference checklist:**

- [ ] Own at least 2 hideouts
- [ ] Build distributor desk before decorating
- [ ] Use parkour to escape raids, not fighting
- [ ] Check notice boards weekly
- [ ] Grind Street Cred alongside money
- [ ] Use low-TOX cutting agents only
- [ ] Buy rural hideout as second location
- [ ] Learn basic parkour moves

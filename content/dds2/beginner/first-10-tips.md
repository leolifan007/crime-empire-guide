The DDS2 tutorial covers basic controls but skips the survival mechanics that experienced players rely on. I picked these up from reading Steam community guides, Reddit discussions, and losing a few hideouts before I figured it out.

**1. Use multiple hideouts early.**

Hideout raid risk is affected by wanted level and visibility. The Fandom wiki confirms helicopters can appear during raids and you can lose stored assets. I split raw materials, finished product, and cash across 3+ hideouts now. Never keep everything in one location.

**2. Parkour is your primary escape tool.**

The wiki states: "Use parkour to find shortcuts, to make it easier to escape from enemies chasing you, or to sneak into otherwise inaccessible areas." Vaulting, wall-running, and climbing break NPC line of sight instantly -- no weapons needed. I stopped fighting police after learning this.

**3. Police heat affects raid probability.**

Your wanted level directly impacts how likely hideouts are to be raided. Heat levels go from None to Person of Interest to Suspect to Wanted. I keep heat low by avoiding gunfire in public, rotating selling locations, and laying low during increased patrol periods.

**4. Two-currency economy.**

DDS2 uses local currency and USD. The Steam page confirms this dual-currency system as a core feature. Buy supplies in local currency and sell finished product in USD -- the exchange differential is where the real profit margin lives.

**5. Recipe cards are physical items.**

Recipes are not auto-unlocked. You find recipe cards in the world as collectible items. The first basic meth recipe is in the tutorial zone warehouse. I always explore new areas thoroughly and check every shelf, desk, and container.

**6. Police hours bring extra cash.**

A Steam community quick-start guide mentions that during peak patrol times, demand increases and customers pay premium prices. I time my production cycles to finish batches just before these windows.

**7. The distributor desk unlocks staff.**

Before you can assign dealers to handle customers, you need to build a distributor desk at your hideout. This is the critical bottleneck -- until you build it, your employees cannot sell product automatically. I made this my first major purchase after the basic lab kit.

**8. Hire through multiple channels.**

According to Steam community discussions, employees come from three sources: notice boards on bulletin boards in town, completing missions for influencers, and story progression. Higher-rarity employees have better stats.

**9. Co-op shares the workload.**

DDS2 supports 3-person co-op. Players can share tasks efficiently: one cooks and manages production, one handles deliveries and street sales, one manages staff assignments. This triples effective throughput per hour compared to solo.

**10. Street Cred unlocks everything.**

"I Am The One Who Knocks" (achievement: Street Cred level 4) is the key early milestone. Higher Street Cred unlocks better recipes, bigger hideout upgrades, more staff slots, and higher-value customer tiers. I prioritize activities that build Street Cred over pure cash grinding.

DDS2 Hideout Locations Guide and Raid Protection

Jun 9, 2026

Hideouts in DDS2 are your operational bases, and they can be raided based on your wanted level and the hideout's visibility rating. I lost my first major stockpile to a raid because I kept everything in the starter shack. The community guide "Pedro Alvarez Real Estate Agency" documents all locations with prices, water/phone line availability, and visibility ratings. This guide covers how to choose hideouts, how raids work, and how to protect your assets.

How raids work:

The Fandom wiki confirms: raid probability is affected by wanted level (None / Person of Interest / Suspect / Wanted) and hideout visibility. Helicopters can appear during raids at higher wanted levels. The raid system checks these factors periodically, not continuously, so there is some randomness in when raids trigger.

Wanted Level Raid Probability Protection Strategy
None Low Standard precautions sufficient
Person of Interest Medium Split assets across 2+ hideouts
Suspect High Hire guards, use hidden compartments
Wanted Very High Minimize time at any single hideout

Practical example: The three-hideout model

After losing a full stash to a raid, I adopted the three-hideout model that the Reddit community recommends:

Hideout Stores Risk if Raided
A (rural/jungle) Raw materials, low-value product Low -- easy to replace
B (industrial) Processing equipment, work-in-progress Medium -- loss of crafting time
C (coastal) Finished product, cash reserves High -- biggest monetary value

If one hideout gets raided, you lose at most one-third of your total operation. I also keep an emergency reserve of approximately $2,000 in cash at each hideout so that a single raid does not leave me broke. This model has saved me from complete wipeouts multiple times.

Visibility rating guide:

Different hideout types have different base visibility ratings. Lower visibility means lower raid probability:

Hideout Type Visibility Best Use
Underground/bunker Very low Primary lab, high-value storage
Jungle cabin Low Cannabis grow, raw material processing
Rural house Low-Medium General production
Industrial warehouse Medium Amphetamine/meth processing
Coastal property Medium Distribution hub
Downtown apartment High Sales hub, client meetings
Villa Very high Endgame status symbol, not for storage
The community consensus from Reddit is clear: if you turn off raids in game settings, you can use any location for space. But with raids enabled (which is how the game is designed to be played), prioritize low-visibility locations for your main lab. A villa might look appealing, but it will attract police attention quickly. Keep your high-value production in rural or underground hideouts.

Hideout protection checklist:

  • [ ] Own at least 2 hideouts before net worth exceeds $5,000
  • [ ] Keep raw materials and finished product in separate locations
  • [ ] Maintain low wanted level before visiting high-value hideouts
  • [ ] Use rural/jungle hideouts for main lab (lower visibility)
  • [ ] Install guard station at highest-value hideout
  • [ ] Keep emergency cash reserve at each location
  • [ ] When raided, escape using parkour rather than fighting
  • [ ] After raid, reduce wanted level before rebuilding stock

DDS2 Raid Prevention: Protecting Your Assets

Jun 9, 2026

Getting raided in DDS2 is preventable. I know because I have been raided several times and each time I could trace it back to something I did wrong. The game's raid system checks wanted level and hideout visibility. Keep both low and your assets stay safe. This guide covers the mechanics behind raids, prevention strategies, and what to do when a raid happens anyway.

Raid cause and effect:

Understanding what triggers raids is the first step to preventing them. The system evaluates these factors:

Factor Impact on Raid Chance How to Control
Wanted level Direct multiplier -- higher level = much higher chance Avoid illegal activity in populated areas
Hideout visibility Location-dependent base rating Choose rural/jungle hideouts for labs
Product volume Indirect -- large stockpiles attract attention Split across multiple locations
Guard presence Reduces probability significantly Hire guards when available
Recent activity Recent crimes in the area increase local patrols Rotate operating zones

Steam Community discussions confirm that raids can only start when you are inside a hideout. The hideout you are currently in is the one that will be targeted. This means you can reduce raid risk simply by spending less time in high-value hideouts.

Practical example: The three-hideout asset distribution model

Here is exactly how I distribute my assets to minimize raid damage:

Hideout Stores Risk if Raided
A (rural jungle cabin) Raw materials, precursor chemicals, low-value product Low -- materials are cheap to replace
B (industrial Callejon unit) Processing equipment, work-in-progress batches Medium -- loss of crafting time, not finished goods
C (coastal hideout with dock) Finished product, cash reserves, high-value items Highest monetary value -- visit least often
I keep an emergency $2,000 in cash and a basic crafting kit at each hideout. This way, even if Hideout C gets raided, I still have the tools to restart production at Hideout A without needing to buy new equipment. The cost of maintaining three basic kits is tiny compared to the cost of a full raid loss.

What to do during a raid:

Per the Fandom wiki: raids can involve helicopters and heavily armed police. When the raid alert triggers, you have options:

  1. Escape (recommended) -- Use parkour to break line of sight. Exit through a rear door or window. Vault fences, wall-run onto roofs, and climb to safety. Police cannot pursue onto most rooftop areas.
  2. Hide (if available) -- Some hideouts have closets, basements, or hidden compartments. Check your hideout layout during setup for potential hiding spots.
  3. Fight (not recommended) -- Police arrive in waves with increasing numbers and equipment. Fighting guarantees your wanted level will increase, making future raids more likely.
  4. Accept loss (if assets are split) -- If you have used the three-hideout model, losing one hideout's contents is an inconvenience, not a catastrophe.

Community consensus from Reddit: fighting during a raid increases your wanted level and makes future raids more frequent and intense. Escape is always the better option.

Post-raid recovery plan:

Step Action Timeframe
1 Check which hideout was hit and assess losses Immediate
2 Transfer remaining assets to safe locations Within 1 hour of gameplay
3 Reduce wanted level (lay low, avoid crime, pay bribes) 1-3 in-game days
4 Rebuild basic equipment at alternate lab 1-2 in-game days
5 Re-establish production at a new or repaired hideout 3-5 in-game days

Permanent raid prevention checklist:

  • [ ] Keep wanted level at None or Person of Interest at all times
  • [ ] Split assets across 3+ hideouts -- never keep everything in one place
  • [ ] Use low-visibility hideouts (rural, jungle, underground) for production
  • [ ] Install guard stations at high-value hideouts
  • [ ] Avoid fighting during raids -- always escape via parkour
  • [ ] Maintain emergency supplies at secondary locations
  • [ ] Visit high-value hideouts only when necessary and for short durations
  • [ ] Rotate operating zones to avoid concentrated police attention in one area

Equipment that reduces raid risk:

Item Effect Approximate Cost
Guard station Assign guards to reduce raid probability ~$5,000
Security cameras Early warning system for incoming raids ~$2,500
Reinforced doors Delays police entry, giving more escape time ~$3,000
Hidden storage Conceals some items from police search ~$1,500
Alarm system Alerts you when police approach the hideout perimeter ~$2,000
Installing security upgrades is worth the investment once your hideout contains more than $10,000 worth of product and equipment. The cost of one upgrade is roughly equal to the value of one lost batch, so if it prevents even a single raid loss, it pays for itself.

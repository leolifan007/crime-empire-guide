DDS2 Raid Prevention: Protecting Your Assets

Updated: Jun 9, 2026

Getting raided in DDS2 is preventable. I know because I have been raided several times and each time I could trace it back to something I did wrong. The game's raid system checks wanted level and hideout visibility. Keep both low and your assets stay safe. This guide covers the mechanics behind raids, prevention strategies, and what to do when a raid happens anyway.

Raid cause and effect:

Understanding what triggers raids is the first step to preventing them. The system evaluates these factors:

FactorImpact on Raid ChanceHow to Control
Wanted levelDirect multiplier -- higher level = much higher chanceAvoid illegal activity in populated areas
Hideout visibilityLocation-dependent base ratingChoose rural/jungle hideouts for labs
Product volumeIndirect -- large stockpiles attract attentionSplit across multiple locations
Guard presenceReduces probability significantlyHire guards when available
Recent activityRecent crimes in the area increase local patrolsRotate operating zones

Steam Community discussions confirm that raids can only start when you are inside a hideout. The hideout you are currently in is the one that will be targeted. This means you can reduce raid risk simply by spending less time in high-value hideouts.

Practical example: The three-hideout asset distribution model

Here is exactly how I distribute my assets to minimize raid damage:

HideoutStoresRisk if Raided
A (rural jungle cabin)Raw materials, precursor chemicals, low-value productLow -- materials are cheap to replace
B (industrial Callejon unit)Processing equipment, work-in-progress batchesMedium -- loss of crafting time, not finished goods
C (coastal hideout with dock)Finished product, cash reserves, high-value itemsHighest monetary value -- visit least often
I keep an emergency $2,000 in cash and a basic crafting kit at each hideout. This way, even if Hideout C gets raided, I still have the tools to restart production at Hideout A without needing to buy new equipment. The cost of maintaining three basic kits is tiny compared to the cost of a full raid loss.

What to do during a raid:

Per the Fandom wiki: raids can involve helicopters and heavily armed police. When the raid alert triggers, you have options:

  1. Escape (recommended) -- Use parkour to break line of sight. Exit through a rear door or window. Vault fences, wall-run onto roofs, and climb to safety. Police cannot pursue onto most rooftop areas.
  2. Hide (if available) -- Some hideouts have closets, basements, or hidden compartments. Check your hideout layout during setup for potential hiding spots.
  3. Fight (not recommended) -- Police arrive in waves with increasing numbers and equipment. Fighting guarantees your wanted level will increase, making future raids more likely.
  4. Accept loss (if assets are split) -- If you have used the three-hideout model, losing one hideout's contents is an inconvenience, not a catastrophe.

Community consensus from Reddit: fighting during a raid increases your wanted level and makes future raids more frequent and intense. Escape is always the better option.

Post-raid recovery plan:

StepActionTimeframe
1Check which hideout was hit and assess lossesImmediate
2Transfer remaining assets to safe locationsWithin 1 hour of gameplay
3Reduce wanted level (lay low, avoid crime, pay bribes)1-3 in-game days
4Rebuild basic equipment at alternate lab1-2 in-game days
5Re-establish production at a new or repaired hideout3-5 in-game days

Permanent raid prevention checklist:

  • [ ] Keep wanted level at None or Person of Interest at all times
  • [ ] Split assets across 3+ hideouts -- never keep everything in one place
  • [ ] Use low-visibility hideouts (rural, jungle, underground) for production
  • [ ] Install guard stations at high-value hideouts
  • [ ] Avoid fighting during raids -- always escape via parkour
  • [ ] Maintain emergency supplies at secondary locations
  • [ ] Visit high-value hideouts only when necessary and for short durations
  • [ ] Rotate operating zones to avoid concentrated police attention in one area

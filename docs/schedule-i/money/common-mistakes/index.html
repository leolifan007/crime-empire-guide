10 Common Schedule I Mistakes That Waste Your Time (And How To Fix Them)

Updated: Jun 18, 2026
Short answer: The most common mistakes are selling during daylight in high-patrol zones, ignoring Dead Drop relationship building, using MK2 Packing Stations with Handlers, and overscoping recipes before upgrading property. Each mistake costs you roughly 2-5 in-game days of progress. Below are all 10 with concrete fixes.

These mistakes are based on real player reports from r/Schedule_I, Steam Community discussions, and community guide feedback. Each entry includes the mistake, why it hurts, and a specific fix.

Mistake 1: Selling During Daytime in High-Patrol Zones

The mistake: Selling on Main Street downtown at 14:00 because it has the most foot traffic.

Why it hurts: Main Street has 2 patrol cars and 1 walking cop during daytime (08:00-18:00). A single sale in view of a cop triggers a chase that costs you 30-90 seconds plus potential lost inventory if caught.

The fix: Sell in the Motel area during daytime (zero patrols, confirmed in all patches v0.3 through v0.4.5). The zone guide breaks down police density by district. Move to higher-traffic zones only at night (22:00-04:00). The 30-second walk to the Motel area saves you more time than you lose in travel.

Mistake 2: Selling All Product Types at Once

The mistake: Carrying weed, meth, and cocaine simultaneously and selling to whoever walks by.

Why it hurts: Each customer type has specific preferences. Selling the wrong product to a customer reduces their buy frequency and can stall customer unlock progression. Multiple Reddit threads confirm this as a common early-game slowdown.

The fix: Focus on ONE product type for the first 2-3 in-game hours. OG Kush is the best starter -- it's cheap, quick to grow, and has the widest customer base. Sell only weed until you hit Hoodlum III rank, then introduce meth. One product at a time until your supply chain is stable.

This is directly from the r/Schedule_I "6 Secrets Schedule 1 Does Not Tell You" post (346 upvotes): "Sell only one type of product in the early game -- it's much easier to manage and helps you maximize your reputation with each customer type."

Mistake 3: Ignoring Dead Drop Relationship Building

The mistake: Ordering supplies from a supplier once and never ordering again, wondering why Delivery never unlocks.

Why it hurts: Only Dead Drop orders build relationship with suppliers. Meeting in person or buying directly does NOT progress the relationship bar. Without building relationship, you're stuck with 10-item order limits and 30-minute delivery times forever.

The fix: Place 1-2 Dead Drop orders EVERY in-game day until the supplier reaches max relationship. The fastest supplier unlock route shows the exact order count needed for each supplier. Order the cheapest item (OG Kush seeds at $10 for Albert Hoover) -- order value doesn't affect relationship gain, only order count does. Once Delivery unlocks, switch to bulk orders.

Mistake 4: Using MK2 Packing Stations With Handlers

The mistake: Buying MK2 Packing Stations because they're "better" and assigning a Handler to them.

Why it hurts: Multiple community reports (Reddit r/Schedule_I, Fandom Wiki, Steam Community) confirm that Handlers on MK2 Packing Stations operate at roughly half the speed of MK1. This was reported as a bug in v0.3.4f8 and has not been fully resolved as of v0.4.5. You're effectively cutting your packaging throughput in half.

The fix: Use MK1 Packing Stations for Handler-based automation. Reserve MK2 stations for manual packing only (when you're actively managing production yourself).

Mistake 5: Overscoping Recipes Before Upgrading Property

The mistake: Trying to run a 6-ingredient cocaine mix in the Bungalow, then wondering why shelves overflow and Handlers drop items.

Why it hurts: The Bungalow supports only 4 employees and limited shelf space (2 Large Storage Racks max). A 6-ingredient recipe requires 3+ shelves just for ingredients. The Handler can't keep up with the route complexity.

The fix: Match your recipe complexity to your property size:

  • Bungalow: 2-3 ingredient recipes only (e.g., Grandmaster Kush with 4 ingredients but you can double-stack on shelfs)
  • Barn: 4-6 ingredient recipes (2 floors for separation)
  • Warehouse/Manor: 6-9 ingredient recipes (unlimited shelf space)

Mistake 6: Not Using the 1.4x Price Rule

The mistake: Selling products at their "suggested" price.

Why it hurts: The suggested price is the minimum. Customers will reliably pay up to 1.4x the suggested price on standard quality products. On premium quality, they'll pay up to 1.6x. You're leaving 30-40% profit on the table.

The fix: Take the suggested price and multiply by 1.4. Round to the nearest $5 or $10. Test with one customer first -- if they refuse, drop by $5-10. This is confirmed by the Steam Community "Pricing Guide For Schedule I" (April 2025).

Mistake 7: Ignoring the 6-Route Handler Limit

The mistake: Assigning 8+ routes to a single Handler because "they can handle it."

Why it hurts: Community testing (Reddit r/Schedule_I, 40+ hour analysis threads) confirms that Handler error rates spike above 6 routes. Errors include: dropping items mid-route, putting ingredients at wrong stations, and freezing at pathing intersections. Each error costs you 2-5 minutes of production downtime while you debug.

The fix: Keep each Handler at 4-5 routes max. If you need more routes, hire a second Handler. This is especially important in the Warehouse where long bay-to-shelf routes increase error probability.

Mistake 8: Buying the Manor Too Early

The mistake: Saving up $250,000 for Hyland Manor because it's the "best" property.

Why it hurts: The Manor is overkill for anyone below Kingpin rank. It needs 16+ employees to run efficiently, which means a $5,500 daily wage bill. Most players never need that much space. The $250,000 could have been spent on two Barns ($60,000) plus full automation setups ($24,000) that generate more total profit.

The fix: Buy the Manor only when:

  • You have all other properties running at max capacity
  • You earn $50,000+/day with current setup
  • You want to run endgame mega-factory operations
  • Otherwise, the Warehouse ($50,000) is more than enough

Mistake 9: Not Diversifying Dealer Customer Assignments

The mistake: Assigning the same type of customers to all 6 dealers.

Why it hurts: Each dealer's customer base has different spending patterns. Assigning high-spending customers to dealers loses you 20% margin on $500 purchases. Assigning low-spending customers to direct sales wastes their high-frequency buying pattern.

The fix: Follow the spending tier rule:

  • S-Tier ($300-500): Sell directly (keep 100% margin)
  • A-B Tier ($80-300): Assign to mid-tier dealers (Brad, Jane, Wei)
  • C-Tier ($30-80): Assign to early dealers (Benji, Molly) -- volume makes up for cut
  • D-Tier ($10-30): Fill empty dealer slots only; sell directly if slots are full

Mistake 10: Not Having a Quick-Escape Property

The mistake: Operating without a safe house near your selling district.

Why it hurts: When a chase starts at Level 3 or 4, your only options are hiding spots (30-90 seconds) or leaving the district (2-3 minutes, blocked by roadblocks). A nearby safe house clears all heat instantly.

The fix: Rent or buy a cheap property ($500-1,000) within 30 seconds of your primary selling area. The Motel room ($500) is perfect -- it's in a zero-patrol zone, close to early-game customers, and clears heat instantly. This single $500 investment saves you more time than any other purchase in the game.

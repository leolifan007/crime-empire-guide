Product Quality vs Price Guide: How Quality Affects Your Profit

Updated: Jun 18, 2026
Short answer: Yes, quality directly affects sell price. Standard quality products sell at 1.4x suggested price. Premium quality sells at 1.5x. Heavenly quality sells at 1.6x. Quality is determined by growing conditions: fertilizer use, PGR/Speed Grow application, and drying rack processing. A Premium OG Kush 4-mix ($170 base) sells for ~$255 at 1.5x, versus ~$238 for Standard -- a $17 difference per unit.

This is a data comparison guide -- not a "what is quality" explainer. Use the tables below to decide whether quality upgrades are worth the extra time and cost. For the base profit figures without quality adjustments, see the recipe profit rankings.

Profit Comparison By Quality

Base assumption: OG Kush 4-mix ($170 suggested price, 4 units per harvest).

Quality Sell Price (per unit) Gross Per Harvest Cost to Achieve Net Per Harvest Extra Profit vs Standard
Standard $238 (1.4x) $952 $0 (baseline) ~$900 $0
Premium $255 (1.5x) $1,020 Fertilizer (~$50) + drying rack time ~$930 +$30
Heavenly $272 (1.6x) $1,088 Fertilizer ($50) + drying rack ($300) + Botanist ($200/day) ~$738 -$162 (net loss without scale)
Heavenly quality is NOT worth the extra cost for small-scale operations. The drying rack ($300) + Botanist (if automated, $200/day) eats into your margin. At 4 units per harvest, you lose $162 per harvest compared to Standard. However, at 20+ units per harvest (Barn scale), the drying rack cost is amortized and Heavenly becomes profitable. The community consensus on r/Schedule_I confirms: "There's no real point in Heavenly unless you want the extra 5-6% chance to get a new customer."

Quality Decision Matrix

Early game (4 pots), need cash fast Standard Quality upgrades cost more than they return at small scale
Mid game (8 pots), Bungalow with Botanist Premium Fertilizer is cheap ($50), drying rack is optional
Late game (Barn, 16+ pots), full automation Premium on weed, Heavenly on cocaine Scale amortizes drying rack cost
Selling to customers directly Premium Higher quality = more customer unlocks, better repeat rates
Supplying dealers Standard or Premium Dealers don't benefit from quality as much as direct sales
Need to unlock a specific customer Heavenly The extra 5-6% unlock chance can be worth it for rare customers

Practical Example

You have a Barn with 20 pots, running Cocaine 8-mix ($625 base). Your Botanist is already hired ($200/day).

Standard quality (no fertilizer):

  • 20 units x $875 = $17,500 gross
  • $17,500 - $200 (Botanist wage) = $17,300 net

Premium quality (fertilizer on all 20):

  • 20 units x $938 = $18,760 gross
  • $18,760 - $200 (Botanist) - $50 (fertilizer) = $18,510 net

Heavenly quality (fertilizer + drying rack, 1 day delay):

  • 20 units x $1,000 = $20,000 gross
  • $20,000 - $200 (Botanist) - $50 (fertilizer) - $300 (drying rack amortized) = $19,450 net

Decision: Go Premium. It adds $1,210 per harvest vs Standard with minimal effort (just apply fertilizer). Heavenly adds another $940 but requires the drying rack purchase ($300) and delays harvest by a day. At Barn scale, the drying rack pays for itself in about 3 harvests.

Community Questions About Quality

From r/Schedule_I discussions (various threads):

Q: Does quality matter for dealer sales? A: Dealers don't see a significant difference in buyer behavior based on quality. The standard pricing rule applies: dealers sell at the price you set. Quality mainly affects direct customer sales and customer unlock rates.

Q: What's the point of Heavenly quality? A: "Currently the highest required quality is Premium, so there's no real point in Heavenly unless you want the extra 5-6% chance to get a new customer." (Steam Community discussion, April 2025). Heavenly is mainly for completionists and min-maxers.

Q: Is fertilizer worth the investment? A: Yes, at medium-to-large scale. Fertilizer costs $50 and adds roughly 7% to your sell price. At 10+ units per harvest, that's $100-300 extra revenue for a $50 investment. (r/Schedule_I "Is fertilizer worth it" thread)

Getting Started in Crime Simulator

Jun 9, 2026

I started Crime Simulator in debt with nothing but a crowbar and a hideout. Took me a few restarts to figure out the flow. Here is what I wish I knew from the start.

The tutorial teaches the basic loop: find a house, break in, steal stuff, sell it, repeat. It is short but do not skip the lockpicking practice -- I spent my first few real runs fumbling with it and getting caught because I rushed past that part. See the tools guide for a breakdown of each tool's pick difficulty.

Your First Job

Pull up the job board at your hideout. Start with the lowest-risk target -- a small suburban house with minimal security. Before you leave:

  • Take the crowbar (your only tool initially)
  • Check the time of day (night gives better cover, I get caught less at night)
  • Note the house layout from the job description

Approach from the rear or sides. Windows and back doors are quieter than the front. Once inside:

  1. Crouch near windows so you do not get spotted from outside
  2. Head toward electronics, cash, jewelry first
  3. Watch for NPCs inside the building -- they patrol on set routes
  4. Exit before the police arrive

Police Alert System

The police system triggers based on noise and visibility. A broken window or a silhouette against a window increases your heat. I learned this the hard way when I smashed a front window in broad daylight and was running before I had even grabbed anything.

What triggers police:

  • Breaking windows without silencing them (glass knife helps)
  • Using the crowbar on doors (loud tool use)
  • Being spotted by NPCs (residents call police)
  • Triggering alarms (security systems in higher-tier houses)

For a complete breakdown of detection ranges and response timers, see the police system guide.

  • Sprinting in the open where neighbors can see you

What is safe:

  • Lockpicking (silent)
  • Using a glass knife on windows (silent)
  • Crouching while moving (reduced visibility)
  • Moving at night (darker = harder to spot)

Selling Your First Haul

Every map has a fence marked with a dollar icon. Drag items from inventory to the sell window. Electronics and jewelry sell for significantly more than household junk -- I grab those first and leave kitchen appliances behind.

A few players on Reddit and Steam suggest keeping one of each common item type early on for item requests. I ignored this advice at first and regretted it when requests popped up for items I had just sold.

After your first heist, keep at least one laptop, one smartphone, and any jewelry you find in your hideout stash. Item requests for these pay 50-100% above fence prices. One saved laptop can mean the difference between making quota and failing.

Tool Priority After the First Run

With your first payout (around $500-$1,000 based on what I and other players have seen), buy in this order:

  1. Basic lockpick set (~$350) -- unlocks locked doors without the crowbar noise
  2. Flashlight (~$125) -- I missed loot in dark corners my first few runs
  3. Sleeping gas (~$225) -- neutralizes a single NPC without alerting others

Skip cosmetic items and hideout furniture early. I bought a poster for my wall my first run and could not afford a lockpick upgrade. Every dollar goes into tools that enable better heists. Decorate your hideout after you have a $5,000 cash buffer and full tool set.

Avoiding the Police

Getting caught means a chase. The Steam guides and Reddit discussions agree on this: break line of sight, hide in a closet or bathroom, stay still until the search timer expires. Do not sprint while hiding -- NPCs can hear footsteps.

Police evasion step-by-step:

  1. Stop moving as soon as you hear the police siren
  2. Find a hiding spot (closet, bathroom, under bed, dumpster)
  3. Stay still and wait for the search indicator to deplete
  4. Wait an extra 10 seconds after the indicator is gone
  5. Crouch-walk to the nearest exit
  6. Do not sprint outside -- walk calmly away from the area

Emergency Save Trick

If you are about to get caught and have not been to the hideout since the start of the run, quit to menu. The game saves at the hideout only. You will respawn with your loot intact. I have used this more times than I want to admit.

This trick only works if you have not visited your hideout during the current run. Once you store items at the hideout, the save point is set. Use this sparingly -- relying on it prevents you from learning proper evasion techniques.

Common Beginner Mistakes

  • Selling gold bars (they fund basement upgrades, worth 5x more as upgrades)
  • Buying hideout decorations before tools (poster vs lockpick -- always the lockpick)
  • Ignoring the item request board (50-100% profit premium)
  • Using the crowbar on every door (loud and gets you caught)
  • Trying to loot every room (greed = arrest)
  • Not checking house intel on the job board (know what you are walking into)

Crime Simulator Game Modes and Quota System Explained

Jun 18, 2026

Crime Simulator has three game modes. The differences are significant -- mode choice determines how punishing the quota is, whether you can die permanently, and how much time you have to explore. Pick the wrong mode and you will restart more than necessary. See the getting started guide to decide which mode pairs best with your first run.

Mode Comparison

Feature Standard Hardcore Completionist
Permadeath No Yes No
Difficulty Normal Master (locked) Variable
Quota Every 3 days Every 3 days No time limit
Quota penalty Warning, then demote Full reset None
Police aggression Normal +50% (community estimate) Normal
Fence prices 100% ~80% 100%
Final score bonus 1x 2x N/A
Best for Learning the game Challenge runs Achievements

The hardcore progression guide maps out what to expect if you jump straight into the hardest mode.

Standard Mode

Standard is the recommended starting mode. Difficulty is set to Normal. Quotas appear every 3 in-game days with a minimum dollar target. Fail the quota and you get a warning. Fail again and your rank decreases. Fail repeatedly and the game ends.

Quota progression in Standard:

  • Quota 1: $500
  • Quota 2: $1,000
  • Quota 3: $2,000
  • Quota 4: $3,500
  • Quota 5-10: increases by $1,500-2,000 each step
  • Quota 10+: $15,000+
The first quota is $500. You can hit this by looting 2-3 small houses and selling everything. Do not waste time on a single high-value target early -- volume is faster in the first 30 minutes.

Dying in Standard sends you back to your hideout. You lose the loot you were carrying but keep your rank and upgrades. This makes it forgiving for learning map layouts, alarm patterns, and police response.

Standard is the fastest way to learn the game. I spent my first 3 hours here and it saved me roughly 5 hours of Hardcore restarts. Learn the map in Standard, then switch to Hardcore once you can clear 3 houses without triggering a single alarm.

Hardcore Mode

Hardcore locks difficulty to Master. Quotas are active with the same 3-day timer, but the penalties are absolute -- miss a single quota and your entire save is reset. Rank, money, upgrades, everything gone.

What changes in Hardcore:

  • Police respond faster and patrol denser routes
  • Alarm systems are more common and harder to disable
  • Fence buy prices are roughly 20% lower (community consensus)
  • Residents are more alert -- footsteps and door noises trigger suspicion sooner
  • The final score is doubled compared to Standard
Do not start Hardcore until you have completed at least one full Standard run to Rank 20+. Hardcore demands complete map knowledge, memorized tool upgrade costs, and reliable alarm-disabling routes. Most new players fail their first Hardcore attempt within the first 3 quotas.

Hardcore survival checklist (before attempting):

  • [ ] Can you reach Rank 12 in Standard without dying?
  • [ ] Do you know which houses have alarm panels and where?
  • [ ] Have you memorized the tool upgrade path (costs and unlock ranks)?
  • [ ] Can you identify high-value loot containers by sight (laptops in home offices, jewelry in bedroom drawers)?
  • [ ] Do you have a reliable escape route from each district?

If the answer to any of these is "no", spend more time in Standard first.

Completionist Mode

Completionist removes the quota timer entirely. There is no deadline -- you explore, loot, and progress at your own pace.

How Completionist works:

  • Quotas still exist but they rise by $500 per run, with no time pressure
  • Difficulty can be adjusted (the mode does not lock it)
  • Objective: complete all available locations by looting everything
  • No permadeath

This mode is designed for achievement hunting and map exploration. Without the quota pressure, you can methodically clear every container, test different tool combinations, and figure out the optimal routes for specific loot types.

**Master thief** (steal 100 items in one game) is easiest in Completionist mode. Start a Completionist run, equip lockpicks and a flashlight, and clear every house district by district. A single large house has 15-25 lootable containers. By house 6 you will have 100+ items. Takes about 40 minutes.

Quota System Mechanics

The quota is the core progression driver. Understanding how it scales lets you plan ahead.

Quota rules:

  • A new quota appears every 3 in-game days (each day is roughly 10-15 minutes real time)
  • The target is cumulative earnings from fence sales and job completion
  • Items stored in hideout do not count -- only items sold
  • Credits earned from completed jobs count toward quota
  • Meeting the quota early does not skip the remaining days -- you still have time to loot and save for the next quota

When you fail a quota:

  • First failure: warning (no penalty)
  • Second failure: rank decrease (-2 ranks)
  • Third failure: game over (save deleted on Hardcore)
If you are close to failing a quota, sell your stored high-value items. One gold bar ($800) or an electronics item ($400-600) can close the gap. Keeping a "quota emergency stash" of 2-3 high-value items in your hideout is a good habit.

Which Mode Should You Play?

  • First time? Standard mode. Learn the maps, tool mechanics, and alarm patterns without pressure.
  • Experienced and want a challenge? Hardcore. It forces perfect execution and punishes every mistake.
  • Going for achievements? Completionist. Most Steam achievements are significantly easier without the quota timer.
  • Want the highest score? Hardcore. The 2x score multiplier means your leaderboard position depends on surviving Hardcore as long as possible.

How to Get Lockpicks in Crime Simulator: Lockpick Set, Bobby Pin and More

Aug 19, 2026

If you have been relying on the crowbar for every locked door, you are making every heist louder and slower than it needs to be. Lockpick tools let you open locks silently -- the difference between a clean escape and a police chase. Here is the complete progression for every lockpick tool in Crime Simulator.

Lockpick tool quick reference: Basic Lockpick Set (buy at shop, basic doors) → Bobby Pin (find in runs, advanced locks) → Advanced Lockpicking skill leaflet (tool boxes/garages) → Master Lockpick Set (world bonus reward, master locks).

Lockpick Tool Progression

Crime Simulator has three tiers of lockpick tools, each unlocking progressively harder locks:

Tool Lock Tier How to Get Cost
Lockpick Set Basic locks Buy at shop Shop price
Bobby Pin Advanced locks Find during runs or buy Varies
Master Lockpick Set Master locks World bonus reward Free after unlock

From basic locks to master locks -- the complete unlock path.

The basic lockpick set handles most doors you will encounter in early-to-mid game houses. The bobby pin is required for higher-security doors with deadbolts. Master locks are rare and only appear in specific endgame locations.

Lockpick Set (Basic)

The lockpick set is your starting tool for silent entry. It replaces the loud crowbar on most interior doors.

How to get it: Buy it from the in-game shop. It is available from the start of a new save.

Use it on: Standard interior doors, bedroom doors, storage closets, and most residential locks.

The lockpick set breaks after several uses and must be repaired or replaced. The tool durability guide covers repair vs replace math.

Bobby Pin (Advanced Lockpick)

The bobby pin opens advanced locks that the basic set cannot touch -- reinforced deadbolts, garage locks, and security doors.

How to get it: The bobby pin is not in the starting shop inventory. Two confirmed paths from community reports:

  1. Find it during runs -- bobby pins appear as loot in tool boxes, garage shelves, and similar containers in mid-to-high tier houses. Multiple Reddit reports confirm finding bobby pins in Lakeside Street runs.
  2. Buy from the Truck Store -- some community posts mention the bobby pin becomes available for purchase at the Truck Store after reaching a certain progression point.

If you are doing runs and not finding bobby pins, try higher-tier neighborhoods. The 201 Masters guide on YouTube specifically recommends the bobby pin for Lakeside Street heists where advanced locks are common.

Advanced Lockpicking Skill Leaflet

The bobby pin tool and the Advanced Lockpicking skill are separate systems. The skill leaflet determines what you can use; the tool is what you have equipped.

To unlock the Advanced Lockpicking skill you need:

  • Skill point -- earned by leveling up through completing jobs
  • Advanced Lockpicking Skill Leaflet -- a collectible found in houses

The leaflet spawns in tool boxes, garage shelves, and locked room containers. Community reports note the Advanced Lockpicking leaflet is not guaranteed per run -- some players went 10+ runs without seeing one. The skill leaflets guide has full spawn location details for all leaflet types.

Pro tip from the community: If you read a skill leaflet and it is not the one you wanted, alt-F4 before saving and quitting to preserve your run progress. When you reload, the leaflet will not be consumed from your inventory, so you can keep trying for the right one.

Master Lockpick Set

Master locks are the highest-tier locks in Crime Simulator. They only appear in specific high-security locations.

How to get it: The Master Lockpick Set is a world bonus reward. World bonuses are permanent, account-wide unlocks earned by completing playthroughs. According to community reports, the fastest players unlock it in around 10-15 runs.

The Master Lockpick Set does not cost money -- once unlocked through world bonuses, it is available on all future saves on your account. The world bonus guide covers the full system including which rewards to prioritize.

Lockpick Tool Strategy

Early game (runs 1-10): Buy the basic lockpick set and rely on it for most doors. Save crowbar for reinforced doors you cannot pick yet.

Mid game (runs 10-25): Hunt for the bobby pin in Lakeside Street and similar high-tier areas. Prioritize finding the Advanced Lockpicking skill leaflet so you can actually use it.

Late game (25+): Focus on world bonus progression to unlock the Master Lockpick Set. Once you have all three tiers, every lock in the game is accessible silently.

Quick reference:

  • Basic lockpick set -- buy at shop, handles most doors
  • Bobby pin -- find in runs or buy at Truck Store, opens deadbolts
  • Advanced Lockpicking skill leaflet -- find in tool boxes/garages, unlocks bobby pin usage
  • Master Lockpick Set -- world bonus reward, opens master locks

How to Unlock Zip Ties in Crime Simulator

Aug 19, 2026

Zip ties are one of the most useful stealth tools in Crime Simulator -- restrain a witness and they cannot reach a phone to call the cops. But they are not in the starting shop, and the unlock method is not obvious. Here is what the community has confirmed.

Short answer: Zip ties are a world bonus reward, not a shop purchase. Complete more runs to unlock higher world bonus tiers. Once unlocked, buy them from the regular shop. They last approximately 5-10 seconds per use -- long enough for a clean escape, not long enough to leave an NPC unattended.

What Zip Ties Do

Zip ties restrain an NPC for the duration of the run. While tied up, they cannot:

  • Call the police
  • Sound an alarm
  • Raise the alert level

They are essential for high-value heists where you need to move through an area without triggering a response. The alternative is knocking them out -- which works but is louder and can trigger witnesses in adjacent rooms.

One limitation confirmed by community testing: zip-tied NPCs will eventually break free after a few seconds (reports suggest around 5-10 seconds). A community mod on NexusMods addresses this by making ties permanent, but in the base game, do not leave a zip-tied NPC unattended for long.

How to Unlock Zip Ties

Confirmed unlock method: Zip ties are a world bonus reward -- earned by completing runs, not bought from a shop.

Community reports from Reddit and Steam discussions agree on the unlock path:

  1. Complete a full run (finish a heist and return to base)
  2. Zip ties appear as a world bonus reward that can be purchased after completing enough runs
  3. Once unlocked as a world bonus, buy them from the regular shop

A Steam community post from July 2025 states: "The cable ties are unlockable at the end of the game, so just keep playing." A Reddit post from 8 months ago confirms: "You unlock it as a reward when you finish a run, and then you can buy it in the shop."

The key detail: you do not find zip ties as loot during runs. They are purely a progression unlock. The more runs you complete, the higher your world bonus tier, and the more items become available for purchase.

World Bonus System Basics

World bonuses are permanent, account-wide unlocks in Crime Simulator. They reset between save files but persist across runs on the same account.

According to the Steam community guide "Understanding Crime Simulator": world bonuses include both items (like zip ties, the crossbow, the invisibility tool) and game features (drop-off boxes, difficulty modifiers).

The fastest players reach 10 world bonuses in around 10 runs. The full list of 25 world bonuses takes longer -- community reports suggest 20 runs for dedicated players.

To check your current world bonus tier, look for the world bonus shop interface when you return from a run. Items you have unlocked will show as available for purchase.

Zip Ties vs Other Restraint Methods

Method Stealth Duration Availability
Zip Ties High Short (5-10s) World bonus
Knock Out Medium Until run ends Starting equipment
Nothing None N/A Default

Complete unlock path: runs build world bonus tier, which unlocks shop purchase.

Zip ties are worth unlocking early. They give you a silent restraint option that knockouts do not -- if you need to move through an area without making noise, zip ties are the tool.

For the full world bonus progression and which rewards to prioritize, see the invisibility tool guide which covers the world bonus system in detail.

Tips

  • Zip ties break after one use -- treat them as consumable, not a permanent tool
  • One community report mentions finding zip ties "just laying around" as rare loot in addition to the world bonus unlock path -- this may be a rare spawn but the primary method is world bonus progression
  • Budget for zip ties in your loadout cost for high-stakes runs where silence is critical

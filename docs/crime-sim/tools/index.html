Crime Simulator Tools Guide: Every Item Explained

Jun 9, 2026

I spent my first few Crime Simulator runs using nothing but the crowbar on every door. Loud, slow, and got me caught repeatedly. Better tools changed everything. The tool upgrades guide explains what each upgrade tier does.

Prioritize the lockpick set first. It replaces the noisy crowbar on most locked doors and pays for itself within 2-3 heists.

Tool Overview

Crime Simulator has a growing arsenal of tools for breaking in, disabling security, and escaping. Tools break with use and must be repaired or replaced. Choosing the right tool for each job is the difference between a clean score and an arrest.

Core Tools

Lockpick set -- The bread and butter of every heist. Lets you open locked doors silently. Without it, you rely on the crowbar, which is loud and alerts nearby NPCs. A simple rotation mini-game: turn the pick left or right until the lock gives. Higher-quality sets handle tough locks better and take longer to break.

Crowbar -- Your starter tool. Works on doors, windows, and crates. Loud. Use it only as backup or for breaking into non-essential containers. I stopped carrying a crowbar after getting the basic lockpick set.

Flashlight -- Many interiors are dark. Without it, you miss loot in corners and on shelves. A must-have for every run. The pro version has a wider beam and longer battery.

For which tools to unlock via skill leaflets rather than purchase, see the skill leaflets guide.

Sleeping gas -- Neutralizes a single NPC silently. I use it when a guard is patrolling a room I need to search. One can per NPC. Do not use it near other NPCs -- they will notice the gas cloud and raise an alarm.

Advanced Tools

Hacking tool -- Unlocked through the Hacking skill leaflet. Opens electronic door locks and security panels. Without it, electronic locks are impassable. The leaflet drops from special loot boxes in mid-to-high security houses.

Glass knife -- Cuts glass silently for window entry. Quieter than breaking glass. I use it on houses with alarmed windows where a broken window would trigger an instant police response.

Cable ties -- Restrain subdued NPCs so they do not wake up and raise alarms. I carry a few for larger houses with multiple NPCs. One cable tie per NPC. Without cable ties, a sleeping gas victim wakes up in about 5 minutes and calls the police.

Late-Game Tools

Drill -- Opens safes. Slow and noisy. Use only when the safe content justifies the risk. I check the safe value through the job board intel before carrying a drill. A typical safe has $200-$800 in cash or valuables.

Blowtorch -- Cuts through reinforced doors and gates. Heavy, noisy, slow. Late-game tool for high-security targets. Also useful for opening safes faster than a drill.

Master Key (Update 1.3) -- Opens most standard residential locks instantly without a mini-game. Does not work on electronic locks or high-security safes. Available through 1.3 content progression.

My Buy Order

  1. Basic lockpick set ($350) -- silent door opening, core progression tool
  2. Flashlight ($125) -- find loot in dark areas
  3. Sleeping gas (1 can, $225) -- NPC emergency removal
  4. Standard lockpick set ($700) -- lower break rate when available
  5. Hacking tool (rush the leaflet) -- electronic lock access
  6. Cable ties ($50) -- for houses with multiple NPCs
  7. Pro flashlight ($250) -- better visibility in late-game houses
Do not buy the drill or blowtorch early. Safes and reinforced doors are rare in the first few districts. By the time you need them, you will have enough income to buy them without sacrificing essential tools. I bought the drill at Rank 2 on my first playthrough and it sat in my stash unused for 10 heists.

Practical Example: Tool Selection for a Mid-Game Heist

I am targeting a medium-security suburban house. The job board says it has an electronic lock on the basement and a safe in the master bedroom.

Tools I bring:

  • Standard lockpick set (main entry, silent)
  • Hacking tool (basement electronic lock)
  • Flashlight (dark basement corners)
  • Sleeping gas (1 can for NPC patrol)
  • Cable ties (2 for restrained NPCs)
  • Drill (safe opening)

Tools I leave behind:

  • Crowbar (noisy, unnecessary with lockpicks)
  • Blowtorch (too heavy, no reinforced doors expected)
  • Glass knife (front door entry, no window needed)

Result: I spend 10 minutes looting everything. The safe had $600 cash. The basement had a laptop ($280). Total haul: $1,100. Clean extraction, no alarms.

Crime Simulator Tool Upgrades and Advanced Tools

Jun 9, 2026

Basic tools get the job done, but upgraded versions save time, reduce noise, and fail less often. I went from breaking a lockpick every 2-3 heists to going 10+ on the professional set. The complete tools guide has the full list of basic tools and their starting stats.

The Master Key from Update 1.3 is the best late-game tool investment I have unlocked. It opens most standard locks instantly, dramatically speeding up heists.

Each tool type has multiple quality tiers. Higher tiers are more expensive but break less often and work faster.

The difference between Basic and Standard is immediately noticeable -- you will break your Basic lockpick on the second or third door of a multi-room house, while a Standard pick handles a whole run. The skill leaflets guide lists perks that reduce tool break rate.

Advanced Tools

Master Key (Update 1.3)

Opens most standard residential locks instantly without a mini-game. Does not work on electronic locks or high-security safes. I use it on every standard door. Combined with the hacking tool for electronic locks, it covers about 80% of entry points.

Where to unlock: The Master Key is part of the Update 1.3 content progression. You need to progress through the 1.3 content chain to access it. It is not purchasable from the standard tool shop. The fast money guide covers how to fund your tool upgrade path efficiently.

Best use cases:

  • Speed runs where every second counts
  • Houses with 5+ standard doors (common in suburban district)
  • Co-op runs where the Lockpick needs to clear doors fast

Hacking Tool

Unlocked through the Hacking skill leaflet. Opens electronic door locks and security panels. Without this, electronic locks stop you cold. I found my first hacking leaflet in a special loot box inside a house with electronic locks.

How to find the hacking leaflet: Special loot boxes (marked with a unique icon) in mid-to-high security houses have the best drop rate. I found mine in the third district on a house with visible electronic locks -- the game seems to place the leaflet near the locks that require it.

Invisibility Tool

Mentioned in developer previews as an expensive tool that makes you invisible for about 60 seconds. Exact requirements are still being refined by the developer. It may not be in all game versions yet. If available, it would be the ultimate panic button for escaping police or bypassing patrols.

The Advanced Lockpicking leaflet and Tool Maintenance leaflet should be your top two priorities. Advanced Lockpicking lets you equip professional-level lockpicks, which are vastly more reliable. Tool Maintenance pays for itself within 10 heists through reduced repair bills.

My Upgrade Priority After Basics

  1. Standard Lockpick Set (~$700) -- reduces break rate significantly. Your first real upgrade. After this, you can complete a full heist without your pick breaking mid-run.
  2. Pro Flashlight (~$250) -- wider beam, longer battery. Worth it for the battery life alone -- a dead flashlight mid-heist means missed loot.
  3. Advanced Hacking leaflet -- unlocks electronic entry. This is the gatekeeper to the high-loot district. Without it, you skip the best houses.
  4. Master Key (Update 1.3) -- skip most lockpicking mini-games. Saves roughly 30-60 seconds per house, which adds up over 20+ heists.
  5. Tool Maintenance leaflet -- saves money on repairs long-term. The earlier you get it, the more it pays back.

The Tool Maintenance skill pays for itself after about 10 heists based on my experience. If you play frequently, hunt for this leaflet before spending on expensive premium tools. On a standard 20-heist run, Tool Maintenance saves approximately $2,000-$4,000 in repair costs alone -- enough to buy the Master Key.

Repair vs Replace Decision

When a tool breaks, you have two options: repair or replace.

ScenarioDecisionReasoning
Basic tool, first breakReplaceRepair costs 50-70% of replacement price
Standard tool, first breakRepairRepair costs 30-40% of replacement
Professional tool, any breakRepairReplacement is 3-4x repair cost
Basic tool, third+ breakReplaceCumulative repair cost exceeds replacement
Any tool near quota deadlineRepairNo time to shop for replacements

I follow this rule: if repair cost exceeds 60% of replacement price, buy new instead. This usually applies to Basic-tier tools where the replacement is cheap.

Storage Tips

  • Keep at least one backup of each essential tool in your hideout stash
  • Mark houses with electronic locks on the map -- they need the hacking tool, not lockpicks
  • Sell broken tools if repair cost exceeds 60% of replacement price
  • Upgrade hideout storage (Update 1.3 basement) to hold more backup tools
  • Store gold bars in hideout upgrades immediately -- never travel with them

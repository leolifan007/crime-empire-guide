Crime Simulator Common Mistakes: 10 Critical Errors New Players Make

Jun 18, 2026

I made every mistake on this list during my first few hours of Crime Simulator. Some cost me a single heist. Others cost me entire Hardcore runs. Here is what I learned so you do not have to learn it the same way.

Mistake 1: Selling Gold Bars for Quick Cash

This is the single most expensive mistake new players make. Gold bars are not loot -- they are currency for basement upgrades. Selling a gold bar for $800-$1,500 seems like great early-game money, but that same bar funds a Storage Rack upgrade that increases your stash capacity by 50%.

The Storage Rack (2 bars) pays for itself within 5 runs by letting you hold more high-value items for requests. The full basement upgrade chain (18 bars total) unlocks powerful permanent bonuses.

Solution: Never sell a gold bar until your basement is fully upgraded. Stash it in your hideout immediately after finding one.

Mistake 2: Buying Hideout Decorations Before Essential Tools

I bought a poster for my wall during my first run. It cost $200 and did nothing for my progression. That $200 could have been a lockpick upgrade that earned $500+ over the next few runs.

Solution: Every dollar in the first 5 hours goes into tools. Lockpick set, flashlight, sleeping gas, cable ties. Decorate your hideout after you have a $5,000 cash buffer and full tool upgrades.

Mistake 3: Ignoring the Item Request Board

The item request board offers 50-100% premium over fence prices. I spent my first 2 hours selling everything to the nearest fence and losing out on hundreds of dollars per run.

Solution: Check the request board before every heist. Store matching items in your hideout. Deliver on your next visit to that map area. Keep 2-3 common request items (laptops, jewelry, smartphones) in storage at all times.

Mistake 4: Using the Crowbar on Every Door

The crowbar is loud. Every door you crowbar has a chance of alerting NPCs and triggering a police response. I lost my first Hardcore run to crowbar noise.

Solution: Buy a lockpick set as your first tool purchase. Use it on every door. Only use the crowbar for breaking crates or as an absolute last resort. The lockpick set pays for itself within 2-3 heists.

LockpickNoneZeroAll standard doors
CrowbarLoudHighEmergency only, crates
Glass KnifeVery lowMinimalWindow entry only

Mistake 5: Trying to Loot Every Room

Greed is the most common cause of arrest. You clear 3 rooms cleanly and think "one more room." That is where the patrol rounds the corner.

Solution: Set a hard limit before entering each house. "I grab 3 items and leave." Or set a time limit. "I am out in 90 seconds." Stick to it. Leave loot behind. Live to heist another day.

Mistake 6: Not Checking Job Board Intel

Every job on the board has a description that tells you what is inside the house. "Home office" means a laptop is likely. "Electronics enthusiast" means multiple high-value electronics items.

Solution: Read the job description before accepting. If it mentions a "tech worker" or "home office," that house is worth prioritizing. If it says "minimal valuables," skip it for a better target.

Mistake 7: Carrying Too Much Loot Before Visiting the Hideout

Every item in your inventory is at risk until you stash it. I once hauled $2,000 worth of loot from 3 consecutive houses without visiting the hideout. Got caught on house 4. Lost everything.

Solution: Visit your hideout after every 2-3 houses. Yes, it takes time to travel back. Yes, it is safer than carrying $1,500+ worth of loot in your inventory. The game saves at the hideout -- use it as a checkpoint.

Mistake 8: Selling Skill Leaflets Before Reading Them

Skill leaflets are one-time unlocks. If you sell a leaflet without reading it first, you might never find that skill again. I sold Tool Maintenance during my first session and did not find another copy for 15 hours.

Solution: Read every skill leaflet immediately when you find it. Even if you plan to prioritize a different skill, reading it first costs nothing. Duplicate leaflets can be sold for $300-$500 each.

Mistake 9: Playing Hardcore Mode Too Early

Hardcore mode deletes your save on arrest. I attempted it after 2 hours of play and lost 4 runs within an hour. The police are more aggressive, quotas are higher, and fence prices are lower.

Solution: Complete Standard mode to at least Rank 12 before attempting Hardcore. You should be able to clear 5+ houses without triggering any alarms. You should know every map layout by memory. See the Hardcore Survival Guide for a readiness checklist.

Mistake 10: Running in the Open During Police Chases

When police are searching, sprinting is the worst thing you can do. Footsteps are audible, movement is visible, and the search area shrinks around your location.

Solution: Hide immediately when you hear sirens. Stay still in a closet or bathroom until the search indicator fully disappears. Wait an extra 10-15 seconds. Then crouch-walk to the nearest exit. Do not sprint until you are at least one block away.

Mistake 11 (Bonus): Not Using the Save Trick

If you are about to be arrested and have not visited the hideout during that run, quit to menu. The game only saves at the hideout. You will respawn at your hideout with all your loot intact.

Solution: Use this sparingly. It is a safety net, not a strategy. If you rely on it too much, you will never learn proper evasion. I allow myself one use per session.

Common Mistakes Checklist

Use this before starting each session:

  • [ ] Have I checked the item request board?
  • [ ] Do I have backup tools in my hideout stash?
  • [ ] Have I read all skill leaflets before selling duplicates?
  • [ ] Are my gold bars safely in the hideout?
  • [ ] Do I know my escape route for the district I am targeting?
  • [ ] Have I spent all cash on useful tools (not decorations)?
  • [ ] Is my hideout storage organized (requests vs. fence items)?

Crime Simulator Endless Mode Guide: Complete Strategy and Survival Tips

Jun 18, 2026

Endless mode in Crime Simulator removes the quota timer and day limit, letting you progress at your own pace. Loot respawns daily in houses you have already cleared. This mode is perfect for learning the game, grinding achievements, and testing strategies without pressure.

How Endless Mode Works

Endless mode was added in Update 1.1. Key mechanics:

  • No quota deadlines -- quotas exist but never expire
  • Loot respawns daily -- houses reset with new loot every game day
  • Full progression -- ranks, tools, skills, and upgrades all carry over
  • Death penalty -- same as Standard mode (lose carried loot, keep rank/upgrades)
  • Police system -- police AI behaves the same as Standard mode
  • Difficulty selection -- choose Casual, Normal, Pro, or Master difficulty

Why Play Endless Mode?

Endless mode is the best choice for several playstyles:

New players: Learn the map layouts, tool mechanics, and police evasion without the pressure of a running quota timer. I spent my first 10 hours here and it saved me from constant restarts.

Achievement hunters: Grind loot-based achievements without worrying about failing quotas. "Millionaire" (earn $1,000,000 lifetime) is significantly easier in Endless mode.

Strategy testing: Try different tool combinations, role assignments, and loot routes without risking a run. If a strategy fails, you lose nothing but time.

Relaxed play: Not every session needs to be a high-stakes heist. Endless mode lets you loot at your own pace.

Early Game Strategy

Your first few hours in Endless mode should focus on building a foundation.

Phase 1: Tool Foundation (First 2-3 Hours)

  1. Complete the tutorial for starter cash and the crowbar
  2. Hit the three cheapest houses on the job board
  3. Buy lockpick set, flashlight, and sleeping gas
  4. Practice lockpicking on every door until the mini-game is muscle memory
  5. Learn the layout of the starter district

Phase 2: Map Mastery (Hours 3-8)

  1. Unlock the suburban district by ranking up
  2. Learn every house layout in both districts
  3. Memorize NPC patrol routes in 5-6 houses
  4. Find your first 2-3 skill leaflets (focus on Tool Maintenance and Stealth Movement)
  5. Save $3,000-$5,000 for tool upgrades

Phase 3: Scaling (Hours 8+)

  1. Target high-security houses with electronic locks
  2. Hunt for rare skill leaflets
  3. Save gold bars for basement upgrades
  4. Begin achievement-focused runs
  5. Experiment with different tool loadouts

Police Evasion in Endless

Since there is no permadeath, you can be more aggressive with police evasion. Unlike Hardcore mode where one mistake wipes your save, Endless mode penalizes you by losing carried loot only.

Endless-friendly evasion tactics:

  • Use the "quit to menu" save trick if you are about to be arrested with valuable loot
  • Practice escaping chases to improve your skills for Hardcore later
  • Take risks you would not take in Standard mode (sprint past NPCs, smash windows for speed)
Endless mode is the best place to practice police chase evasion. Trigger a chase deliberately and practice losing cops. The skills you develop here transfer directly to Standard and Hardcore modes.

Loot Respawn Mechanics

Houses respawn loot every game day. The loot type and value are randomized, meaning a house that had low-value kitchen appliances yesterday could have a laptop today.

Respawn rules:

  • Houses reset at the start of each game day
  • Loot variety increases with your rank (higher ranks = better loot spawns)
  • Safes respawn with new contents daily
  • Special loot boxes respawn with potential skill leaflets
  • NPC positions and patrol routes are also randomized daily

This means you cannot rely on a single house for consistent high-value loot. I rotate through 10-15 houses per session, hitting the ones with the best observable loot.

When to Leave Endless Mode

Endless mode is a training ground, not a permanent home. Move to Standard or Hardcore mode when:

  • You can clear any house in the first two districts without triggering alarms
  • You have all 10 skill leaflets learned
  • You have $10,000+ cash reserve and full tool upgrades
  • You know NPC patrol routes by memory
  • You can escape a police chase without using the quit trick
  • Standard mode quotas feel easy or boring

Crime Simulator House Tier Guide: Map Districts and Loot Value

Jun 18, 2026

Crime Simulator has multiple districts, each with different house types, security levels, and loot potential. Knowing which district to target at each stage of the game saves you time and reduces risk.

House Types by District

Starter Town (Rank Apprentice)

Small, single-story houses with minimal security. No electronic locks, few NPCs, low-value loot. Best for learning the basics.

Common house layouts:

  • Small apartment (2 rooms): 1 bedroom, 1 living room. Loot in kitchen and bedroom. Easy clear in 60 seconds.
  • Suburban cottage (3 rooms): Living room, kitchen, 1 bedroom. Bathroom cabinet often has cash.
  • Corner house (4 rooms): Larger but still low security. NPC patrols are predictable.

Typical loot: Kitchen appliances, basic clothing, small electronics, occasional smartphone.

Suburban District (Rank 3-5)

Two-story houses with moderate security. Electronic locks start appearing. Better loot, especially electronics and jewelry.

Common house layouts:

  • Two-story family home: Ground floor living/kitchen, upstairs bedrooms. Master bedroom has best loot.
  • Split-level house: Entry on mid-level, stairs up and down. Multiple hiding spots.
  • Modern townhouse: Narrow but tall, 3 floors. More containers but tighter patrol routes.

Typical loot: Laptops, tablets, jewelry, designer clothing, cash.

The Suburban district is the sweet spot for mid-game grinding. Houses have reliable electronics loot without the high police response time of Downtown. I farmed 80% of my mid-game wealth here.

Industrial District (Rank 6-9)

Warehouse-style buildings with larger interiors and multiple entry points. Less residential, more commercial.

Common house layouts:

  • Warehouse conversion: Open floor plan, lots of hiding spots, fewer NPCs but higher chance of security systems.
  • Workshop house: Garage area with tools and equipment, living quarters in back.
  • Office-residence combo: Business front, living space behind. Safes are common.

Typical loot: Tools, power equipment, silverware, large electronics (TVs, game consoles).

Downtown District (Rank 10+)

High-security urban buildings. Tight streets, fast police response, but the best loot in the game outside of heist targets.

Common house layouts:

  • Luxury apartment: High-rise with doorman. Limited entry points, short escape routes.
  • Penthouse suite: Top floor, multiple rooms, excellent loot. Highest risk of alarm systems.
  • Brownstone townhouse: Narrow multi-floor buildings. Lots of containers but tight corridors.

Typical loot: Gold bars, authentic jewelry, high-end electronics, large cash stacks.

Downtown houses have the best loot-per-container ratio in the game, but the police response time of 20-30 seconds means you have almost no margin for error. I only hit Downtown when I have full tool upgrades, the Stealth Movement skill, and a pre-planned escape route. One alarm in Downtown is almost certainly an arrest.

Heist Locations (Rank 12+)

Special map locations marked with a briefcase icon. These are not standard houses -- they are dedicated heist targets with unique layouts and very high-value loot.

Heist types:

  • Bank heist: Vault with cash, electronic locks, multiple guards
  • Museum heist: Display cases with authentic jewelry, motion sensors
  • Mansion heist: Large estate, gold bars in basement safe, patrol routes
  • Jewelry store: Display cases, alarms, fast police response

Practical Example: Progression by District

Here is my recommended progression path for a Standard mode playthrough:

  1. Heists 1-5: Starter Town. Learn lockpicking, buy basic tools.
  2. Heists 6-15: Suburban. Farm electronics, find first skill leaflets.
  3. Heists 16-30: Industrial. Target safes and tool-heavy houses.
  4. Heists 31-50: Downtown. High-risk, high-reward for major cash.

By following this progression, you never take on a house harder than your current gear can handle. Each district prepares you for the next one.

Crime Simulator Police System Guide: AI Behavior and Evasion

Jun 18, 2026

The police system in Crime Simulator is the primary fail state. Understanding exactly how it works -- what triggers it, how fast police respond, and how to evade them -- is the difference between a clean score and a deleted save in Hardcore mode.

How Police Detection Works

Police are triggered by a heat system. Every action you take generates heat. When your heat reaches a threshold, police are called.

Heat-generating actions:

  • Breaking windows without a glass knife (high heat)
  • Using crowbar on doors (high heat)
  • Being spotted by NPCs (instant high heat)
  • Triggering house alarms (very high heat)
  • Sprinting in view of neighbors (moderate heat)
  • Making noise near patrol routes (moderate heat)
  • Leaving doors open (low heat over time)

Heat-reducing actions:

  • Staying still in darkness (slow reduction)
  • Hiding in closets or bathrooms (fast reduction)
  • Leaving the area (full reset after 60 seconds)
  • Completing a heist cleanly (resets for next heist)

Police Response Times

Police response varies by district and difficulty. In Standard mode on Normal difficulty, expect:

Starter Town45-60 seconds+15 sec per alertHide in nearby house
Suburban30-45 seconds+10 sec per alertClosets, bathroom

In Hardcore mode, police response is approximately 50% faster across all districts. Downtown response drops to 10-15 seconds. If you trigger an alarm in Downtown on Hardcore, you have less than 15 seconds to hide before police arrive. I learned this the hard way.

Police AI Behavior

The police AI follows a predictable pattern once dispatched:

Phase 1: Dispatch

Police spawn at district entry points. They head toward the triggered location. During this phase, you have roughly 30-60 seconds to hide or leave the area.

Phase 2: Search

Police arrive at the location and begin searching. They check rooms systematically. If they find an open door or broken window, they enter the building.

Search behavior:

  • Police check rooms in a set order (ground floor first, then upstairs)
  • They listen for movement noise (sprinting and running are detected)
  • They check closets and bathrooms if the room is dark
  • They leave after approximately 60 seconds if nothing is found
  • They search with flashlights at night, making dark corners less safe

Phase 3: Chase

If police spot you, a chase begins. Chases escalate in intensity:

  • 1 star: One police car, simple evasion possible
  • 2 stars: Two cars, tighter search grid
  • 3 stars: Helicopter joins, very hard to escape

The key insight about police AI is that they respond to noise, not to you directly (unless an NPC spots you). If you are hiding silently in a closet and police enter the room, they will check the closet only if they have reason to believe someone is there. Stay still. Do not even shift your crouch position. I have been in a closet while a cop walked past it three times because I stayed completely still.

Evasion Map: Best Hiding Spots by District

Knowing where to hide is essential. Here are reliable hiding spots for each district:

Starter Town:

  • Closets in any house (best option)
  • Bathroom stalls (if you entered a house with a bathroom)
  • Behind garden sheds (exterior, risky)
  • Under beds in master bedrooms

Suburban:

  • Walk-in closets (large, easy to enter)
  • Basement corners (dark, hard to spot)
  • Garage storage areas
  • Behind large furniture (couches, cabinets)

Industrial:

  • Warehouse containers (excellent, dark)
  • Dumpsters (reliable but smelly)
  • Behind machinery (good cover)
  • Rooftop corners (if accessible)

Downtown:

  • Alley dumpsters (most reliable)
  • Apartment building hallways (enter any open door)
  • Rooftops (if you can access via fire escape)
  • Underground parking (dark, multiple exits)

Step-by-Step Evasion Protocol

  1. Stop moving the moment you hear a siren or see a police car
  2. Identify your nearest hiding spot (closet, bathroom, dumpster, alley)
  3. Crouch-walk to the hiding spot (do not sprint -- footsteps are audible)
  4. Enter the hiding spot and hold still
  5. Watch the search indicator -- it pulses while police are searching
  6. Wait for the indicator to fully disappear plus 15 extra seconds
  7. Crouch-walk to the nearest exit
  8. Exit the district if possible (cross-district travel resets police)

How to Avoid Police Entirely

The best strategy is never triggering police in the first place.

Golden rules:

  • Always use lockpicks, never crowbars, for door entry
  • Use glass knives for window entry on alarmed houses
  • Move at night when possible (darker = harder for neighbors to spot you)
  • Close doors behind you (reduces visual evidence)
  • Check the street before exiting (wait for patrols to pass)
  • Never sprint in front of windows
  • Disable alarm panels before looting (faster than suppressing alarms)
The single most effective police avoidance tactic is closing doors behind you. An open door is visible from the street and tells patrols someone is inside. It takes 2 seconds to close a door and can prevent an entire police response. I close every door I open, even interior doors.

Practical Example: Avoiding a Close Call

I hit a suburban house at night. Inside, I found a laptop ($280) and cash ($120). On my way out, a patrol car turned onto the street. Instead of running out the front door, I:

  1. Moved to a back window (verified no patrols visible)
  2. Used the glass knife to cut the window silently
  3. Dropped into the backyard
  4. Crouch-walked along the fence line
  5. Exited through the neighbor's yard to the next street over

Total delay: 45 seconds. Police never entered the house because I closed the front door behind me and exited through the back. They circled the block twice and left.

Crime Simulator Safe Houses Guide: Best Hideouts and Upgrades

Jun 18, 2026

Your hideout is more than just a place to store loot. It is your operations center, your safe zone, and the key to long-term progression. Choosing the right upgrades and managing your storage effectively can double your earning potential.

Hideout Basics

Your hideout serves several critical functions:

  • Storage: Keep loot between runs for item requests or bulk selling
  • Safe zone: Police cannot enter your hideout -- get here and you are safe
  • Save point: The game autosaves when you visit your hideout
  • Job board: Accept heist contracts and item requests
  • Upgrades: Basement expansion (Update 1.3) unlocks powerful bonuses

Storage Management

Your hideout has limited storage space. Managing it well is essential.

Storage management rule: keep high-value items for requests, sell everything else immediately. Your hideout is not a museum -- it is a staging area. I keep 2-3 laptops, 2-3 jewelry pieces, and 1-2 gold bars. Everything else gets fenced immediately.

Update 1.3 added a hideout basement expansion system. Gold bars (a new resource) are used to purchase upgrades that provide permanent bonuses.

The Storage Rack is the highest-impact upgrade per gold bar spent. Two gold bars for +10 storage slots means you can hold 10 more high-value items for request delivery. At an average premium of $100-$200 per request item, that is $1,000-$2,000 extra per game session.

How the Security Door Works

The Security Door upgrade protects your stash when you are arrested. Without it, you lose everything in the hideout on arrest (Hardcore) or lose a portion (Standard). With the Security Door, your stored loot is protected.

This is a critical upgrade for Hardcore mode players. In Standard mode it is less urgent but still valuable for long-term progression.

Tool Stash Management

Keeping backup tools is cheaper than buying replacements after a failed heist.

Recommended backup stash:

  • 1 backup lockpick set (get caught without one and you cannot enter houses)
  • 1 backup flashlight (dead flashlight = missed loot)
  • 1 sleeping gas (emergency NPC removal)
  • 2-3 cable ties (NPC restraint)

Storage strategy for tools:

  • Dedicate 4-5 storage slots to backup tools
  • Rotate tools: when your primary breaks, grab the backup and repair the primary
  • Never dip below one backup of each essential tool

Hideout Layout Tips

While you cannot redesign your hideout, you can organize your storage efficiently:

  • Left side of stash: High-value items for requests (laptops, jewelry, gold bars)
  • Right side of stash: Backup tools and equipment
  • Bottom rows: Bulk items you plan to fence soon
  • Tool wall (if upgraded): Display your best tools for quick access

Practical Example: Storage Optimization

Here is my actual stash layout after 10 hours of gameplay:

Permanent storage (always kept):

  • 2 gold bars (saving for basement upgrades)
  • 2 laptops (request items)
  • 3 jewelry pieces (request items, authentic)
  • 1 set of backup tools (lockpick, flashlight, gas, cables)

Temporary storage (sold within 2 runs):

  • Any duplicates of requested items
  • Extra skill leaflet duplicates
  • Mid-value electronics if storage is full

This setup lets me accept any item request that appears, while keeping my stash lean enough to add new loot without needing to sell mid-run.

Co-op Hideout Sharing

In co-op mode, all players share the same hideout. Storage is shared. This requires coordination:

  • Designate one player as the "stash manager" who organizes items
  • Pool high-value items for requests before individual selling
  • Communicate what you are storing so duplicates are minimized
  • Avoid storing items that another player plans to sell immediately
Co-op hideout conflicts are common. I recommend one simple rule: each player gets 5 dedicated storage slots. The rest is shared pool. This prevents one player from filling the stash with low-value items while others have nowhere to store gold bars.

Crime Simulator Skill Leaflets Guide: Complete List and Priority Order

Jun 18, 2026

Skill leaflets are the backbone of progression in Crime Simulator. Unlike tools that you buy with cash, skill leaflets must be found during heists and then activated with skill points earned from ranking up. Choosing the right leaflet priority can save you dozens of hours.

How Skill Leaflets Work

Each skill leaflet unlocks a permanent ability or upgrade. To activate a leaflet, you need:

  • The leaflet item (found during heists)
  • One skill point (earned by ranking up)

You can find duplicate leaflets. Once you have learned a skill, duplicate leaflets sell for approximately $300-$500 at the fence -- a decent income source, especially in the early game.

Never sell a leaflet you have not read yet. Even if it seems low priority, reading it first costs nothing. If you sell it before reading, you might never find that leaflet again for 20+ runs. I made this mistake with Tool Maintenance and regretted it for hours.

I have found that Tool Maintenance, Advanced Lockpicking, and Stealth Movement form the "core three" leaflets that every player should prioritize. Together they reduce tool breakage, enable better tools, and make you harder to detect. The remaining leaflets are nice-to-have but not essential for beating Standard mode.

Best Priority Order

Based on my playthroughs across Standard and Hardcore modes, here is the optimal order:

Tier 1: Must-Have (Get These First)

  1. Tool Maintenance -- Reduces tool break rate. This saves you money on repairs and prevents mid-heist tool failures. Without it, you will break a lockpick every 2-3 houses. With it, every 5-7 houses.
  2. Advanced Lockpicking -- Unlocks professional lockpick sets. Better lockpicks mean quieter entry, fewer breaks, and faster mini-games.
  3. Stealth Movement -- Reduces detection radius. This is the difference between getting caught and walking past a patrol.

Tier 2: Strong Boost (Get After Core)

  1. Carry Capacity -- More loot per run. Simple and effective. One extra slot translates to roughly $150-$300 extra per run.
  2. Hacking -- Opens electronic-locked rooms. These rooms have higher-value loot. Without this leaflet, you skip the best houses.
  3. Stealth Ops -- Quieter tool usage. Good for high-security houses where noise carries further.

Tier 3: Situational (Get When You Can)

  1. Jewelry Appraisal -- Only useful if you frequently hit high-security houses with jewelry. Nice convenience but not game-changing.
  2. Tool Crafting -- Requires the workshop bench upgrade. Crafting is cheaper than buying but the bench costs 8 gold bars.
  3. Speed Boost -- Useful for police chase achievements but rarely needed in normal play.
  4. Extra Pocket -- Another inventory slot. Stack with Carry Capacity for maximum looting efficiency.

Where to Find Skill Leaflets

Leaflets spawn in specific container types and locations. Here is what I have found most consistent:

Common spawn locations:

  • Dressers and nightstands in every house (most common spawn)
  • Kitchen drawers and countertops
  • Bedroom closets
  • Bathroom cabinets (often overlooked)

Uncommon spawn locations:

  • Garage shelves and workbenches
  • Living room sideboards
  • Shelves near safes
  • Home office desks

Rare spawn locations:

  • Special loot boxes (marked with unique icon)
  • Locked rooms (require hacking tool to access)
  • Heist target locations
  • Basement containers in high-security houses
If you are specifically hunting for a rare leaflet like Jewelry Appraisal or Speed Boost, focus on high-security houses with special loot boxes. Standard suburban houses rarely spawn rare leaflets. I found Jewelry Appraisal in a display case inside a house with electronic locks -- the game seems to place it near the mechanic it affects.

Skill Point Economy

You earn one skill point per rank up. There are approximately 25 ranks, meaning you get 25 skill points total. Since there are 10 skill leaflets, you have enough points for all of them with 15 to spare for any future additions.

However, ranking up takes time. By Rank 10, you will have 10 skill points -- just enough for the 10 current leaflets. Plan your first 5-6 points carefully because later ones take progressively longer to earn.
